About this site

This site is an interactive guide to the probability distributions that are most often used in statistics and in computational modeling, and to the ways in which they are related to one another. It is meant for students and researchers who want to see how a distribution changes as its parameters change, to look up its formulas and properties, and to understand where it comes from and what it is used for.

What is on the site

  • The map (the home page) shows the distributions as tiles and their relationships as arrows. Clicking a tile opens the page for that distribution. Clicking an arrow or its label opens a short explanation of the relationship, with a link to a page where you can explore it.
  • Distribution pages all have the same sections: a short description; an interactive plot of the density (or, for counts, the probability mass function) next to the cumulative distribution function; the formulas, with every symbol explained; a table of properties such as the mean, variance and skewness; limits and special cases; uses in frequentist statistics, in Bayesian statistics and in modeling; related distributions; and references.
  • Relationship pages explain one connection between two distributions, such as a special case, a limit, a transformation or a conjugate prior. Each has a plain explanation, the formal statement, a glossary of the symbols, an interactive demonstration and a short explanation of why the relationship holds.

How to use the interactive plots

  • Handles that you can drag are drawn in amber. A dashed vertical line usually sets a location such as the mean; a horizontal arrow usually sets a scale such as the standard deviation. Curves are drawn in blue.
  • Each parameter also has a number box. Dragging a handle updates the box, and typing a value in the box updates the plot. If a value is outside the allowed range, a short message explains what is allowed and the plot keeps the last valid value.
  • A handle can also be selected with the Tab key and moved with the arrow keys (hold Shift for larger steps).
  • The area under the curve switch shades the area to the left or right of a point, or between two points, and shows its value. That area is a probability. The same number appears on the cumulative distribution plot as the height of the curve at that point (or one minus that height for the area to the right), which shows how the density and the CDF are connected.

How it is built

The site is a set of static pages with no server-side code. Plots are drawn as SVG with a small plotting library written for the site, and all probabilities, quantiles and special functions are computed in the browser. Formulas are typeset with KaTeX. The source code is on GitHub; corrections and suggestions are welcome as issues there.
